# Paper-Cut Illustration

Keep the photographic setting and reinterpret only the requested person as a clearly cartoon-like hand-drawn paper cutout.
This mode favors source placement and silhouette over caricature distortion. Use a permitted source photograph;
an existing synthetic portrait is suitable for a development example when labeled as such.

## Preserve The Scene Structure

Default to the original image's aspect ratio, framing, camera angle, subject size, pose, gaze, and placement.
Preserve the outer silhouette, limb proportions, visible hand positions, contact points, and foreground occlusion.
Gaps between arms and body or between legs must still reveal the scene behind them. Do not draw hidden anatomy
through furniture or turn an object the person touches into part of the paper figure.

A user-requested reframe or scene change overrides only that choice. Do not silently apply the usual 4:5 social crop.
Exact photographic background preservation is a separate delivery claim: use masking/compositing when available and
verify unaffected areas. If the surface redraws the whole image, report observed changes or the inability to guarantee
pixel preservation; do not describe prompt-level preservation as an exact edit.

## Draw Inside The Preserved Silhouette

Default to a simple picture-book cartoon, not a realistically shaded portrait surrounded by a white edge.
Use the Impression Map to retain the head outline, hair silhouette, feature placement, expression, and pose;
recognition here comes from these economical cues rather than recreating every facial plane or age line.
Keep adult body proportions when the source is an adult; simplification does not require a baby face or a larger head.

Use small dot or short oval eyes, simple brow strokes, a tiny nose mark, and a short expressive mouth line when
consistent with the requested expression and view. These marks are the paper-cut default, not a rule for the
watercolor or caricature modes. Do not add realistic irises, eyelid shading, modeled lips, pores, or wrinkle inventories
just to satisfy a generic likeness instruction. A user may request more facial detail without changing other choices.

Draw with clear, lightly irregular contours and broad flat matte color areas. Keep skin largely one tone; optional
cheek color is a small accent, not a full facial shading system. Group hair into a few dark shapes with sparse direction
lines. Reduce clothing to its silhouette, main colors, and a few lines that explain folds or overlap. Avoid fabric weave,
knit loops, stippling, all-over paper grain, and dense painterly shading inside the figure. Hands remain simple outlined
shapes with readable contact boundaries. Preserve the original clothes and palette rather than importing another
reference person's outfit.

Give the figure a narrow, slightly irregular white paper margin with at most a faint contact shadow. Keep that margin
secondary to the drawing and do not bridge real openings, cover foreground objects, or create a thick sticker halo.
Keep the surroundings photographic unless the request explicitly includes them.

## Inspect And Revise

Check source geometry and contact/occlusion first, then identity cues, drawing economy, paper edges, and background changes.
At thumbnail size the person should read immediately as a cartoon. If facial modeling or texture still dominates,
remove that detail; adding a paper border alone does not satisfy this mode.
Correct one named defect while keeping accepted choices. Use the consistency reference to distinguish a fresh variation
from an edit to the accepted artwork. Do not restart a successful local correction from scratch merely to obey a slogan.

Deliver the actual artwork, dimensions, and any material preservation limit. A multi-expression sticker sheet is a
separate layout request, not this mode's default output or a guarantee that each tile can be edited independently.
