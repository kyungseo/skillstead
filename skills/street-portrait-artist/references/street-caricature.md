# Street Caricature — Exaggerate

Create the feeling of a skilled, anonymous street-fair artist who found one strong idea quickly and drew it with warmth.
The portrait may be boldly transformed, but it should remain affectionate, readable, and specific to the subject.

## Structural Contract

- Build from the shared Impression Map, not from a generic oversized-head template.
- Let the primary anchor dominate the design. Keep other features quieter and use action-reaction compensation to
  maintain a coherent face.
- Allow stronger changes to head frame, T-axis spacing, mouth-chin rhythm, expression flow, or silhouette when they are
  supported by the references.
- Preserve the recognizable expression, hair logic, eyewear, facial hair, and other outer anchors unless the design
  explicitly depends on simplifying them.
- Aim for “clearly this person, seen through one clever idea,” not anatomical copying or maximum distortion.

## Construction Before Finish

Use two distinct passes when the image surface supports an intermediate image or a targeted edit:

1. `Construction Study`: establish the head envelope, center and eye axes, T-axis, mouth-chin rhythm, outer-anchor
   silhouette, primary anchor, and its action-reaction response. Use sparse searching contours and simple value masses.
   Do not finish the eyes, lips, skin, hair strands, clothing texture, or photographic shading in this pass.
2. `Ink Final`: use the original portrait as identity correction and the accepted study as structural authority. Add
   expressive line weight, open-paper planes, decisive black masses, and only the detail that supports recognition.
   Rendering must not pull the face back toward anatomical copying or an enlarged-head photograph.

Before the final pass, compare the study with the original reference. The head frame, T-axis, mouth-chin rhythm,
expression, and primary anchor must still identify the subject. If the construction is wrong, revise the study rather
than hiding the error under polished ink. When the surface cannot carry an intermediate image, include the same
construction constraints in one generation and inspect structure before accepting finish quality.

## Material And Mark Contract

- Surface: warm white or lightly toned drawing paper with visible but restrained fiber.
- Marks: brush pen, black ink, felt tip, graphite, or a compatible mixed sketch language.
- Line hierarchy: thin and broken on lit or tentative edges; firm and heavy on lower contours, overlaps, under-chin
  shadow, nostril base, eyelid accents, and other small occlusion zones.
- Mass: use a few decisive black shapes and sparse hatching. Keep much of the face as open paper.
- Color hierarchy: default to `90–96%` near-monochrome ink and graphite. Treat the paper as the face's main light and
  skin tone instead of painting a broad flesh wash. A tiny muted ochre, olive, rust, or similarly subdued spot color is
  optional only when it reinforces an identity-bearing garment or outer anchor.
- Color restraint: do not broadly color the face, hair, or clothing and do not let spot color become a second visual
  system. Use full color only when the user explicitly requests it, while preserving the ink-first structure.
- Finish: preserve searching strokes, slight asymmetry, incomplete contours, and a hand-made edge. Avoid uniform vector
  outlines, smooth digital gradients, plastic skin, or a photographic base with an ink overlay.
- Scene: subject-first blank paper by default. A few non-text gesture marks are optional; no detailed environment unless
  explicitly requested.

## Cuteness And Dignity

Cuteness comes from compact visual economy, lively expression, and confident simplification—not automatically from
larger eyes, a childlike body, smaller nose, or infantilization. Do not exaggerate disability, age, skin condition, or a
feature the user asks to leave neutral. If a requested exaggeration risks ridicule, offer a gentler anchor or ask for the
desired intensity.

## Failure Signals

- Every feature is enlarged equally, so no primary idea exists.
- The result resembles a generic mascot, animation franchise, or stock big-head caricature more than the subject.
- A single feature is pasted larger while surrounding relationships remain unchanged.
- Photographic shading or skin survives under decorative ink lines.
- The result reads as a colored realistic portrait, watercolor-photo render, or photo with an enlarged head rather
  than a redrawn relational caricature.
- Dense crosshatching, black fill, or background detail buries the face and expression.
- The construction study already looks like a finished portrait, leaving no clear relational design for the ink pass.
- The final rendering restores photographic proportions and keeps only an oversized head as the caricature signal.
