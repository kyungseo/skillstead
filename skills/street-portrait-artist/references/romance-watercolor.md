# Romance Watercolor — Illuminate

Create a delicate pen-and-watercolor character portrait with restrained idealization and real watercolor behavior. The
mode should reveal the subject's recognizable visual rhythm through graceful selection and emphasis, not replace the
person with a generic idealized character.

## Structural Contract

- Start from the same Impression Map used by Street Caricature. Preserve the head frame, T-axis, mouth-chin rhythm,
  expression, and outer anchors that make the subject recognizable.
- Illuminate rather than aggressively distort: simplify planes, clean the silhouette, group hair flow, and selectively
  emphasize the eyes or expression only where the map supports it.
- Favor elegant proportion and flowing direction without automatic de-aging, beautification, enlarged eyes, narrowed
  jaw, erased texture, standardized anime features, or compulsory romance-comic facial conventions.
- Clothing may be simplified into graceful folds and larger wash families, but keep identifying silhouette and colors
  when they matter.

## Material And Mark Contract

- Surface: cold-pressed watercolor paper with visible, non-uniform grain.
- Drawing: light pencil, colored pencil, or fine ink underdrawing with varied pressure and occasional lost contours.
- Paint: transparent layered washes, blooms, granulation, backruns, uneven edges, and reserved paper highlights.
- Face: fewer marks than the reference, selective line emphasis, gentle color transitions built from pigment rather
  than airbrushed gradients, and no plastic skin smoothing.
- Hair: grouped directional masses with a few descriptive strands; avoid strand-by-strand photographic rendering or
  hard cel-shaded chunks.
- Scene: retain meaningful environmental anchors as soft broken washes when they contribute to the portrait. Keep the
  person more defined than the environment and allow paper gaps and lost-and-found edges.

## Character Idealization Without Style Copy

Use generic qualities such as lyrical line, expressive eye-axis, airy negative space, quiet color harmony, gentle
dramatic framing, precise varied pen contours, and selective facial simplification. A romance-comic feeling is optional,
not the required facial template. Do not imitate a named artist, studio, franchise, or existing character design. If the
request names one, translate it into generic visual qualities and preserve the user's own visual identity.

## Failure Signals

- The result is a near-photograph with paper texture or color wash laid over it.
- The face becomes a generic idealized protagonist and loses the subject's spacing, jaw, mouth, hairline, or expression.
- Digital cel shading, glossy 3D lighting, uniform outlines, or synthetic skin replaces watercolor physicality.
- The environment is rendered leaf by leaf, removed despite carrying narrative context, or competes with the face.
- “Romantic” becomes a personality or relationship claim rather than a visual treatment.
