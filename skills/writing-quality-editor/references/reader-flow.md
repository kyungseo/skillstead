# Reader Flow

Use for multi-paragraph new drafts, document-level assessments, or an already justified structural revision.
A sentence polish, short reply, or sound existing structure does not need this pass. The source meaning and
Revise change boundaries still govern; this reference cannot authorize a wider rewrite.

## Follow The Reader's Questions

Privately identify the question each paragraph answers and the answer it contributes. A paragraph may give
context, evidence, a comparison, a qualification, or an action; it need not contain an explicit question.
Read the sequence for the intended audience:

- Does a paragraph contribute information needed for the reader's task, or repeat an earlier answer?
- Does a conclusion have its reason or evidence, and can the reader distinguish observation from interpretation?
- Does a transition rely on a relationship that the supplied material establishes but the prose leaves unclear?
- Are conditions and warnings available before the reader must act on them?

Repair only an observed problem. Move or combine supported material when that is enough; do not invent an
incident, failed attempt, counterargument, motive, causal bridge, or lesson to make the sequence interesting.
If a necessary answer is absent from the evidence, retain that uncertainty or ask for the missing fact.
A clear outline, a chronological account, and an expected document template are not defects. Do not add
surprise, digressions, uneven paragraph lengths, or a quota of opinions for their own sake.

Do not expose the question map as a routine deliverable. In Assess, report only actual findings with a source
anchor and reader impact. In Revise, changing paragraph order still requires the structural-revision gate.

## Match The Document's Job

For a technical article or experience report, connect the concrete problem, the available evidence, the author's
actual decision, and the conditions that limit it. Spend detail where it helps the reader assess that decision.
Use a failed attempt or personal experience only when supplied; an explanatory article need not become a story.
Keep a genuine open question open rather than manufacturing a concluding lesson.

For a review or issue reply, answer the question or state the finding first when context permits, give the
necessary evidence, and make any required action clear. If no judgment or action was requested, do not invent one.
Host templates, source anchors, and approval boundaries keep their authority.

## Check The Cost Of An Edit

Within the allowed edit spans, consider whether added wording resolves a named reader problem. Remove padding;
retain words needed for grammar, context, accessibility, a condition, or a meaningful relationship. Revert a
replacement when the original already does the job and the change only expresses a preference. Shorter is not
always better. Do not enforce word-count, sentence-length, punctuation, or replacement/deletion ratios.
