# Korean Compose

Use this contract for new Korean writing from supplied facts, notes, a brief, or reviewed research.
Follow the requested reader, purpose, medium, and tone. The user's explicit corrections determine which facts
are authoritative. Ask only when missing information changes required content or meaning; do not require an
existing prose draft, a writing sample, or a form to begin.

## Establish What Must Survive

Before writing, identify the material facts and commitments: who does what, conditions and timing,
numbers, obligation strength, causal relationships, exceptions, uncertainty, limitations, risks, and the
author's actual position or experience. Preserve these relationships as well as the individual facts.
Keep fact, observation, inference, proposal, and personal opinion distinguishable.

Keep commands, identifiers, link targets, direct quotations, and explicitly protected text exact unless
the user requested a change to those items. Keep quotations with their attribution and qualifications.
Do not change a linked heading unless the request includes maintaining its references.
Preserve the format that carries meaning, such as code blocks, ordered steps, or a table's associations.

Respect the requested document scope and any explicit formatting constraints.
Do not add a product capability, procedure, approval rule, event, quote, or personal experience to make prose fluent.
Ordinary references can be resolved from clear local context; do not demand information the text does not need.


## Check The Brief And Compose

Before drafting, check whether the supplied or reviewed facts support the material claims the requested text
must make. If a required fact is missing, do not draft that factual account, including a narrative skeleton with
placeholders. Give a supported standalone passage only if it serves the request, and ask neutral questions for
the missing facts. Do not assume whether an unreported outcome improved or worsened, or suggest plausible answers
as if they supplied the missing evidence. State when the requested account cannot yet be completed.

When the facts are sufficient, choose a suitable structure and write the useful text directly. You may create
wording, ordering, headings, and connections that express the material; this does not authorize new asserted facts
or operational steps. A named artifact alone does not establish how a user must operate it. Use reviewed sources
when the task needs research, following the host's research and citation requirements. Cover the required content,
without forcing a template or producing an intentionally rough draft for another pass.


## Korean Expression

Use familiar Korean phrasing appropriate to this reader and medium. Express the supplied action or relationship
directly when compressed nouns or an abstract metaphor make the reader reconstruct the point.
Preserve useful technical terms; explain them when the audience needs an explanation supported by the material.
Do not invent an actor or condition to complete a smoother sentence.

Write the reader's actual subject, not a narration of the writer's editorial reasoning. Do not carry internal
review shorthand into reader-facing prose: descriptions of how to classify, separate, elevate, or limit a claim
can be accurate as working notes yet awkward as the finished explanation. State what happened, what was checked,
what remains untested or unknown, who acts, or what the reader should do, as appropriate to the passage.
Apply this while drafting, not only after the user points out an awkward sentence.

For example, a verification note should tell the reader which environment was checked and which checks remain,
using the author's supplied context. Do not merely exchange an abstract verb for a synonym or append a natural
explanation after leaving the awkward sentence in place. Recast the complete defective clause or sentence.
Treat the author's approved replacement as the intended meaning and register; do not reopen facts it explicitly
settles. When a fact is genuinely absent, preserve that uncertainty in ordinary language without inventing it.
Keep an actual reviewer instruction when the reader is being instructed to review; this is a reader-purpose
check, not a ban on technical terms or a requirement to expand already clear prose.

These describe different commitments, not stylistic alternatives:

- `설정을 임의로 바꾸지 않는다.` prohibits discretionary changes.
- `유지보수자 승인 없이 설정을 바꾸지 않는다.` requires prior approval.
- `사용자에게 알리지 않고 설정을 바꾸지 않는다.` promises notification.

If only `조용히 바꾸지 않는다` is supplied and context does not resolve the intended commitment, do not choose
one of these meanings for the new text. Ask which commitment the brief intends when the text needs that fact.

Remove a stock introduction, repeated heading explanation, forced contrast, or inflated conclusion only when it
adds no needed meaning or misrepresents the material. A pattern often used by AI is not automatically a defect.
Keep a useful contrast, a clear metaphor, or a genuine opinion. Do not use banned-word or punctuation lists.
Where provided, use the author's sample to understand directness, formality, sentence length, and information order;
do not copy distinctive phrases, invent a persona, or supply experiences the author did not provide.


## Check The Draft And Deliver

For a multi-paragraph draft, apply `references/reader-flow.md` to the supported material. Check what each
paragraph contributes before polishing its wording; do not force a narrative or print a routine question map.


Before delivery, reread any sentence that describes a judgment about the subject instead of the subject itself.
If the reader must translate it into “무엇을 했고, 무엇은 하지 않았다는 뜻인가?” or “그래서 무엇을 해야 하나?”,
replace it with that direct explanation using the established facts. Do this check even when the sentence is
grammatically correct and preserves the claim boundary.

Check the draft against the supplied facts, commitments, exact items, and required brief content. Correct or remove
unsupported claims before returning it. Do not hide a missing required fact by giving the prose a fluent ending.
Distinguish a complete text from supported material that cannot yet satisfy the whole request.

Lead with the usable text when one can be written. If essential facts are missing, identify them directly rather
than giving a narrative template that assumes those facts. Include needed citations and concise questions.
A routine preservation ledger, source-notes section, or audit inventory is not required. Follow the host's format
for a file change and explain a meaningful structural decision briefly when it helps the user review the result.
